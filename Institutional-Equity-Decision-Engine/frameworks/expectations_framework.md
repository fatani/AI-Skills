# Expectations Framework

## Objective

Understand that stock prices move based on changes in expectations, not absolute results.

The key question is not:

"Is the company performing well?"

The key question is:

"Is the company performing better or worse than expected?"

---

## Consensus Expectations

Determine:

* Revenue Expectations
* Earnings Expectations
* Margin Expectations
* Guidance Expectations
* Market Narrative Expectations

Questions:

* What does the market currently expect?
* What assumptions are embedded in the stock price?

---

## Expectation Gap

Identify:

* Positive Surprise Potential
* Negative Surprise Potential

Questions:

* What would exceed expectations?
* What would disappoint expectations?

---

## Expectations vs Reality

Evaluate:

* Current Reality
* Market Expectations

Determine:

* Reality > Expectations
* Reality = Expectations
* Reality < Expectations

---

## Multiple Expansion Analysis

Questions:

* What could justify a higher valuation multiple?
* What could improve investor sentiment?
* What could attract additional institutional capital?

---

## Multiple Compression Analysis

Questions:

* What could reduce investor confidence?
* What could damage the narrative?
* What could trigger institutional selling?

---

## Variant Perception

Identify:

* Consensus View
* Alternative View

Questions:

* What does the market believe?
* What might be true instead?

---

## Earnings Reaction Framework

Evaluate:

* Reported Results
* Guidance
* Future Expectations

Remember:

A stock can fall on good results.

A stock can rise on bad results.

Expectations matter more than results.

---

## Final Assessment

Determine:

* Current Expectations Level
* Probability of Positive Surprise
* Probability of Negative Surprise
* Most Important Expectation Embedded In The Stock
