# Evidence Framework

## Objective

Separate facts from estimates, assumptions, opinions, and unknowns.

A conclusion is only as strong as the evidence supporting it.

Strong reasoning built on weak evidence remains weak.

---

## Evidence Classification

Every major statement must be classified as one of:

### Fact

Definition:

Directly supported by verifiable evidence.

Examples:

* Reported revenue
* Reported earnings
* SEC filings
* Company guidance
* Confirmed ownership data

Confidence Level:

Very High

---

### Estimate

Definition:

Reasonable approximation based on available information.

Examples:

* Market share estimates
* Industry forecasts
* Analyst consensus estimates

Confidence Level:

Medium

Must be labeled:

[Estimate]

---

### Assumption

Definition:

Required belief without direct verification.

Examples:

* Future demand growth
* Competitive responses
* Margin sustainability

Confidence Level:

Low to Medium

Must be labeled:

[Assumption]

---

### Opinion

Definition:

Interpretation of evidence.

Examples:

* Attractive valuation
* Strong management
* Superior strategy

Confidence Level:

Variable

Must be labeled:

[Opinion]

---

### Unknown

Definition:

Insufficient evidence available.

Examples:

* Missing current data
* Unverified rumors
* Future regulatory actions

Confidence Level:

Low

Must be labeled:

[Unknown]

---

## Evidence Quality Assessment

For every major conclusion determine:

* Evidence Strength
* Evidence Recency
* Evidence Reliability
* Evidence Completeness

Score:

1 = Very Weak

2 = Weak

3 = Moderate

4 = Strong

5 = Very Strong

---

## Contradictory Evidence

Always ask:

* What evidence contradicts this conclusion?
* What evidence would invalidate this thesis?

Never rely on supporting evidence alone.

---

## Missing Evidence Analysis

Identify:

* Critical missing information
* Unknown variables
* Data requiring verification

Questions:

* What do we not know?
* What would change the conclusion?

---

## Confidence Calibration

Confidence must be based on evidence quality.

Not conviction.

Not narrative strength.

Not popularity.

Rule:

Weak evidence = Low confidence

Strong evidence = High confidence

---

## Research Gap Identification

Identify:

* Data gaps
* Research gaps
* Verification requirements

Label clearly:

[Requires Verification]

---

## Final Assessment

For each major conclusion provide:

* Conclusion
* Evidence Type
* Evidence Quality Score
* Confidence Level

Always ask:

"How much of this conclusion is evidence, and how much is inference?"
