# Expert Debate Framework

## Objective

Force the analysis to consider opposing viewpoints before reaching a conclusion.

Avoid confirmation bias.

Avoid narrative traps.

Avoid one-sided thinking.

---

## Bull Case

Construct the strongest possible bullish argument.

Questions:

* Why could the stock outperform expectations?
* What is the market underestimating?
* What positive catalyst could emerge?
* What could drive multiple expansion?

---

## Bear Case

Construct the strongest possible bearish argument.

Questions:

* Why could the stock disappoint?
* What assumptions may be wrong?
* What negative catalyst could emerge?
* What could drive multiple compression?

---

## Market Expectations

Determine:

* What is already priced in?
* What is not priced in?

Markets react to changes in expectations.

Not absolute results.

---

## Institutional Debate

Simulate two professional investors:

### Investor A

Bullish

Must provide:

* Thesis
* Evidence
* Risks

---

### Investor B

Bearish

Must provide:

* Thesis
* Evidence
* Risks

---

## Reconciliation

After both arguments:

Identify:

* Strongest Bull Argument
* Strongest Bear Argument
* Most Likely Outcome
* Highest Conviction Insight

---

## Final Principle

Never allow a conclusion before both sides have presented their best case.
